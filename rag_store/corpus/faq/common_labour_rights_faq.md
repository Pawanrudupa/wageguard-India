# Frequently Asked Questions on Indian Labour Rights & Wage Law

## FAQ 1: Can my employer delay or withhold my final salary after I resign?
- **Short Answer**: No. Under Indian law, an employer cannot legally delay or withhold your final salary after you resign.
- **Statutory Authority**: Payment of Wages Act, 1936, Section 5(2).
- **Explanation**: Section 5(2) of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936 explicitly mandates that where an employee's employment is terminated or where an employee resigns and separates from employment, all earned wages must be paid before the expiry of the second working day (2nd working day) from the day employment ceases. Company policies requiring "45 to 90 days for full and final settlement" cannot override this statutory requirement.
- **Permissible Deductions**: Under Section 7(2) and Section 7(3), the employer can only make authorized deductions (such as statutory PF/tax or documented unreturned company equipment with prior notice), and total deductions cannot exceed 50% of the wage.
- **Where to Complain**: Payment of Wages Authority / Labour Court under Section 15 of the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, or file an online grievance on the Shram Suvidha portal.

## FAQ 2: Is my employer allowed to make deductions from my salary without my consent?
- **Short Answer**: No. Unauthorized deductions are strictly illegal under Indian labour law.
- **Statutory Authority**: Payment of Wages Act, 1936, Section 7, Section 8, Section 9, and Section 10.
- **Explanation**: Section 7(1) mandates that wages must be paid without deductions of any kind except those explicitly authorized under the Act (such as statutory Provident Fund, income tax, court attachments, or absence from duty). Section 7(3) imposes a strict statutory ceiling: total deductions cannot exceed 50% of total wages in any wage period (or 75% for co-operative societies).
- **Fines**: Under Section 8(4), fines cannot exceed 3% of the monthly wage and can only be imposed after issuing a show-cause notice.
- **Absence**: Under Section 9(2), deduction for absence cannot exceed the proportionate wage for the days absent (e.g. deducting 2 or 3 days' pay for 1 day of absence is illegal).
- **Remedy**: Section 15 allows workers to recover unauthorized deductions with compensation up to ten times (10x) the amount deducted.

## FAQ 3: Am I entitled to overtime pay if I work more than 8 or 9 hours a day?
- **Short Answer**: Yes. If you work beyond the standard working hours, your employer is legally required to pay you overtime at double your regular wage rate.
- **Statutory Authority**: Minimum Wages Act, 1948, Section 14, and Factories Act, 1948, Section 59.
- **Explanation**: Under Section 14 of the Minimum Wages Act, 1948 and Rule 25 of the Minimum Wages Rules, any worker who works beyond normal hours (ordinarily 8 to 9 hours in a day or 48 hours in a week) must be paid overtime at twice the ordinary rate of wages (2x normal hourly rate: basic wage + dearness allowance). Offering compensatory off instead of overtime pay, or paying overtime at single normal rate, is a statutory violation.
- **Remedy**: Claims for unpaid overtime can be filed before the Authority under Section 20 of the Minimum Wages Act within 6 months (extendable with cause).

## FAQ 4: What can I do if my employer deducts PF from my salary but does not deposit it?
- **Short Answer**: This is an illegal practice and constitutes a criminal offence under Indian law.
- **Statutory Authority**: Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952, Section 14, and Indian Penal Code (IPC), Section 405/406 (Criminal Breach of Trust).
- **Explanation**: Employers must deposit the employee's contribution (12%) together with the matching employer's contribution to the EPFO within 15 days of the close of the wage month. Deducting the contribution from the worker's salary slip and retaining or using the funds is punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years.
- **Where to Complain**: File an immediate grievance on the EPFiGMS portal (https://epfigms.gov.in) with salary slips showing the deduction, or report to the Regional PF Commissioner.

## FAQ 5: When am I eligible for Gratuity and how is it calculated?
- **Short Answer**: You are eligible for gratuity if you have completed at least 5 years of continuous service with the same employer.
- **Statutory Authority**: Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, Section 4 and Section 7.
- **Formula**: `Gratuity = (15 * Last Drawn Monthly Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance * Years of Service) / 26`.
- **Payment Deadline**: Under Section 7(3), the employer must pay the gratuity within 30 days of it becoming payable. If delayed beyond 30 days, the employer must pay simple interest (mandated at 10% per annum under Section 7(3A)).
- **Cap**: Maximum statutory tax-free gratuity is ₹20 Lakhs under S.O. 1420(E).
- **Remedy**: Apply to the Controlling Authority (Assistant Labour Commissioner) under Form N.

## FAQ 6: What is the statutory minimum wage, and can I agree to work for less?
- **Short Answer**: You cannot waive your right to statutory minimum wages; any contract agreeing to less than the notified minimum wage is legally void.
- **Statutory Authority**: Minimum Wages Act, 1948, Section 12 and Section 25.
- **Explanation**: Section 12 obligates employers to pay not less than the state-notified minimum wage for the worker's scheduled employment and skill level (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled). Under Section 25, any contract or agreement whereby an employee relinquishes or reduces his right to a minimum rate of wages is completely null and void. Paying less than minimum wages constitutes forced labour / wage theft punishable under Section 22.
