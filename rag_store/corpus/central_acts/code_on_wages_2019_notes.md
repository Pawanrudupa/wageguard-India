# Code on Wages, 2019 (Act No. 29 of 2019) — Implementation Status and Key Provisions

## Statutory Status Caveat (Crucial Legal Notice)
The Code on Wages, 2019 was enacted by Parliament on 8 August 2019 and received Presidential assent on 8 August 2019 (Gazette Notification Act No. 29 of 2019).
IMPORTANT STATUTORY STATUS NOTICE:
As of 2024, although the Central Government published draft Central Rules under the Code on Wages, 2019, the full operational enforcement date has NOT been universally notified across all States in India because Labour is a concurrent subject (Entry 24 of List III, Seventh Schedule to the Constitution of India).
Under the existing constitutional framework, until the appointed date is notified by the Central Government repealing the predecessor Acts:
1. The Minimum Wages Act, 1948 remains the primary operational statute in all States.
2. The Payment of Wages Act, 1936 remains the active enforcement mechanism for wage deductions and delay.
3. The Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 and Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 continue in operational force.
Any legal navigator or rights assistant must advise workers based on the existing operational statutes (Payment of Wages Act 1936, Minimum Wages Act 1948) while providing the Code on Wages 2019 provisions as future statutory benchmarks.

## Section 6 & 9: Universal Floor Wage and Minimum Wage
The Code on Wages, 2019 consolidates 4 existing labour laws:
(1) The Payment of Wages Act, 1936;
(2) The Minimum Wages Act, 1948;
(3) The Payment of Bonus Act, 1965;
(4) The Equal Remuneration Act, 1976.
Key consolidations:
- Statutory Floor Wage (Section 9): The Central Government is empowered to fix a National Floor Wage taking into account the minimum living standards of a worker. State Governments cannot fix minimum wage rates lower than the National Floor Wage.
- Universal Coverage (Section 6): Unlike the Minimum Wages Act 1948 which applied only to "scheduled employments", the Code on Wages removes the scheduled employment restriction and applies minimum wage mandates to all workers across organized and unorganized sectors throughout India.

## Section 17: Time Limit for Payment of Wages
- In monthly wage periods, wages must be paid before the expiry of the 7th day of the succeeding month.
- Section 17(2) Final Settlement: In case of removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation, or termination of employment, wages earned must be paid within two working days (2 working days) of the removal, dismissal, resignation, or termination.

## Section 18: Permissible Deductions Under the Code
- Authorised deductions remain strictly controlled (fines, absence, house accommodation, provident fund, tax, advance recoveries).
- Total deductions in any wage period continue to be capped at fifty per cent (50%) of the total wages of the employee. Deductions above 50% remain illegal.
