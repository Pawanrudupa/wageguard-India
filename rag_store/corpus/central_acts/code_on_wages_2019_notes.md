# Code on Wages, 2019 (Act No. 29 of 2019) — Implementation Status and Key Provisions

## Statutory Status Notice (In Force Effective 21 November 2025)
The Code on Wages, 2019 was enacted by Parliament on 8 August 2019 (Gazette Notification Act No. 29 of 2019).
OFFICIAL COMMENCEMENT NOTICE:
The Code on Wages, 2019—along with the Industrial Relations Code, Social Security Code, and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code—was officially notified and brought into force by the Central Government on **21 November 2025** via Official Gazette notification.

### Important Transitional Nuance and Active State Rules
While the substantive provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019 are in force:
1. **Rule-Making Status**: Central and several State-specific Rules under the Code were actively being finalized as of early 2026. Draft Central Rules were published for public consultation and comments around 30 December 2025.
2. **Concurrent Subject Administration**: Under Entry 24 of List III (Concurrent List) of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution of India, both Parliament and State Legislatures hold jurisdiction over labour. Where specific State-level administrative rules have not yet been gazetted, full operational detail and procedural claims may still rely on transitional application of the predecessor statutes (Payment of Wages Act, 1936; Minimum Wages Act, 1948).
3. **Mandatory Freshness Verification**: Because administrative rules, notifications, and state guidelines were actively evolving as recently as this corpus's knowledge cutoff (valid as of 2026-01-01), any user or legal navigator must re-verify specific administrative mechanisms against the India Code portal (indiacode.nic.in) or the relevant State Labour Department gazette before filing formal proceedings.

## Section 6 & 9: Universal Floor Wage and Minimum Wage
The Code on Wages, 2019 consolidates 4 historical central labour laws:
(1) The Payment of Wages Act, 1936;
(2) The Minimum Wages Act, 1948;
(3) The Payment of Bonus Act, 1965;
(4) The Equal Remuneration Act, 1976.

Key substantive reforms:
- **Statutory National Floor Wage (Section 9)**: The Central Government is empowered to fix a National Floor Wage taking into account basic living standards. State Governments are legally prohibited from fixing state minimum wage rates lower than the National Floor Wage.
- **Universal Coverage (Section 6)**: The Code eliminates the historical "scheduled employment" restriction from the 1948 Act. Statutory minimum wage protection now applies universally to all workers across organized and unorganized sectors throughout India.

## Section 17: Time Limit for Payment of Wages (In Force Since 21 Nov 2025)
- **Monthly Wage Periods**: Wages must be paid before the expiry of the 7th day of the succeeding month.
- **Section 17(2) Final Settlement on Resignation or Termination**: Where an employee is removed, dismissed, retrenched, or resigns from service, or where employment is otherwise terminated, the wages earned by the employee must be paid within **two working days (2 working days)** of such removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation, or termination.

### Statutory Resignation Settlement Rule:
Under Section 17(2), the two-working-day statutory settlement deadline explicitly applies to voluntary resignation as well as employer-initiated termination. This substantively resolves the historical gap in Section 5(2) of the Payment of Wages Act 1936 (which had explicitly addressed only employer-initiated termination).
*Operational Caveat*: While this statutory right is in force, local dispute resolution procedures and enforcement authorities are transitioning from the older 1936 Act mechanisms to the Code's adjudicative authorities. Workers facing delayed settlements should assert Section 17(2) while also referencing state Shops & Establishment Act rules where state Code procedural rules are still finalizing.

## Section 18: Permissible Deductions Under the Code
- Authorised deductions remain strictly restricted to statutory heads (fines, absence, house accommodation, provident fund, income tax, advance recoveries).
- **Statutory 50% Ceiling**: Total deductions in any wage period continue to be capped at fifty per cent (50%) of the total wages of the employee. Deductions in excess of 50% remain illegal.
